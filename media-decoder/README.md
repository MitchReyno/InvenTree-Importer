# Media decoder

The program that runs inside the pinned, network-less media container. It decodes one retained original (image or PDF) into normalized pages, crops and WebP renditions, and writes a framed result to stdout. It never touches the database, the blob store or the network.

- `src/decoder-main.ts` is the container entry point; it reads one request from stdin.
- `src/decode.ts` and `src/rotated-region.ts` do the decoding with `sharp`, `pdfjs-dist` and `@napi-rs/canvas`.
- `src/view.ts` is `render_view`, the `media-view/1` operation behind question pictures. It renders one view of a retained page PNG (a photo or a decoded PDF page) from a `bench-intake-view/1` recipe: it cuts the region, straightens it with the corner guard, applies quarter turns and resizes to the sizes `viewLayout()` in `@invimport/contracts/media` gives. It returns a WebP view and a WebP thumbnail. When marks are sent, `src/view-marks.ts` also draws them onto a copy as a PNG preview for the agent; the view itself never carries marks. Labels use `LiberationSans-Bold.ttf` from `pdfjs-dist`'s standard fonts, registered at runtime, because the image has no system fonts.
- `src/view-pixels.ts` is the one geometry path every view and every recipe `/2` crop takes (the older `documentary-crop/1` and `documentary-rotated-region/1` derivations keep theirs in `src/decode.ts` and `src/rotated-region.ts`): it cuts the straightened window with the affine sampler in `src/rotated-region.ts`, applies quarter turns and resizes to the layout. `viewLayout(recipe, 'full')` keeps a region's full resolution, as derived crops and their previews need.
- `src/lit-crop.ts` renders recipe `/2` (`bench-intake-view/2`), the view recipe with light adjustments, at full resolution, and `src/light.ts` applies them. White balance uses grey-world gains bounded to ±15%; a hue guard scales them back towards 1 in eighths until no saturated pixel (HSV saturation of at least ½ and channels at least 48 levels apart) turns more than 5° (2.5° when luminance adjustments follow), and refuses the recipe when even an eighth would. Contrast (autocontrast), gamma and sharpening change only luminance, scaling each pixel's channels together, so only rounding can turn a hue. Contrast, gamma and sharpening protect any pixel whose brightest channel and channel spread are both at least 16 levels, where hue is meaningful in 8 bits: they keep its spread at 12 levels or more and rebuild its middle channel from the rounded ends, so rounding turns its hue at most 2.5°. Below that floor a hue isn't protected, and darkening may turn or erase it. When they follow white balance, the guard allows white balance the remaining 2.5°, so the light step as a whole turns no saturated pixel more than 5° from its source. A `media-decode/1` request with a recipe `/2` derives the crop as a lossless PNG; one that doesn't fit its page, or whose white balance is refused, is `malformed`. `render_view` (`media-view/1`) also takes a recipe `/2`, without marks: it lights the crop at full resolution and then sizes it to the view.
- `src/preview.ts` is `preview_images` (`media-preview/1`): up to eight recipe `/2` crops on up to four retained page PNGs, each page naming the evidence version, digest and page it was decoded from, which every recipe on it must match. Each crop is rendered as its derivation would be, on one labelled montage (montage.py's 360 px cells, four across). It reports each crop's size, its flags (`corner_guard`, `white_balance_held_back`) and gains, or that the hue guard refused its white balance. Only that refusal is per crop: a missing or conflicting page, a recipe naming another page, or a region outside its page makes the whole preview `malformed`.
- `src/grid.ts` is `inspect_region` (`media-grid/1`), ported from image-grid-overlay's grid.py: a grid labelled in page pixels over a page or region, with proposed boxes, their centre crosshairs and their margins to the object, flagged as clipping or off-centre as the script flags them. Each line and its label sit on the same preview pixel, kept inside the preview. Labels go on every line, or on every 2nd, 5th, 10th … line when they would otherwise crowd; axis labels and box names are laid out together and drawn over the lines and outlines. Each box name goes at the first of its box's inner corners, or just outside its top or bottom, where all its ink (measured glyph bounds, accents included) fits the preview and covers no other name; failing that, or when the name draws no ink, its number in the request is drawn instead, and each box reports the `drawn_label` it got (null if neither fitted). An axis label that won't fit the preview or whose chip would intersect another is left out, and the result lists only the labels drawn.
- `src/logo-match.ts` is `match_logo` (`media-logo-match/1`), ported from chip-logo-identification's match.py: a view of a page, turned upright with the shared geometry, shrunk to at most 1024 px on its long edge, and ranked against the bundled logo set in `src/chip-logos/` (a byte-for-byte copy of the skill's `data/index.json` and `data/logos/`, checked by a test), with a candidate sheet.
- `src/decoder-protocol.ts` holds the request and result types and the limits. It is the only module the intake server imports, as `@invimport/media-decoder/protocol`.

The intake server builds the image (`intake-server/scripts/build-media-image.cjs`), runs it (`intake-server/src/media/decoder-runner.ts`) and parses its output (`intake-server/src/media/decoder-output.ts`). See the [retained media boundary](../intake-server/ARCHITECTURE.md#retained-media-and-additive-attachment-execution) for the container limits and the worker.

Run `pnpm --filter @invimport/media-decoder test` for the decoding tests. The container itself is qualified by the intake server's process tier.

The ports are tested against the skills' own scripts on fixtures in `tests/fixtures/parity/`, which `tests/fixtures/parity/generate.py` regenerates (run it from the repository root as its docstring shows). Crops and quarter turns match the scripts pixel for pixel, and so does white balance wherever neither the ±15% bound nor the hue guard changes crop.py's gains (crop.py allows 0.8–1.25). Straightened crops match within interpolation tolerance, because Pillow rotates with a cubic of a = −1 and libvips with Catmull-Rom.
